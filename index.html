The Problem

Two pollution crises,
one solution

Stubble burning and plastic scrubbers are separate problems creating compounding harm. Our product addresses both at once.

01
🔥

Parali burning

Farmers burn 35 million tonnes of paddy stubble annually after harvest in Punjab and Haryana. This creates a toxic smog blanket over Delhi every October–November — PM2.5 levels spike to 400+.

149 million tonnes CO₂ released/yr nationwide
02
🧽

Plastic scrubbers

Every kitchen scrubber is made from non-biodegradable plastic or nylon. They shed microplastics into your food and water with every use — microplastics have now been found in human blood and organs.

2.5 kg CO₂ emitted per kg of plastic made
03
🌾

Why farmers burn

The rice-wheat cropping cycle leaves only 2–3 weeks between harvest and next sowing. Burning is the fastest, cheapest way to clear fields. Silica coating on parali makes it unusable as cattle feed.

20 million tonnes from Punjab alone, 80% burned
04
💨

Health impact

Stubble burning contributes to an estimated 44,000–98,000 premature deaths per year in India. Rural Punjab residents spend ₹7.6 crore per year on treatments for respiratory illnesses caused by smoke.

AQI 300–400 in Delhi during burning season
Our Solution

The Parali Scrubber

A kitchen scrubbing pad made from rice stubble and natural binders — tackling both the burning problem and the plastic problem simultaneously.

Step 01 — Raw material

Sourced from stubble

Parali is collected from Punjab farms post-harvest instead of being burned. Chopped into short segments, washed with boiling water to soften fibres, then sun-dried.

20M tonnes available in Punjab alone
Step 02 — Processing

Natural binding

Rice starch (amylopectin from cooking water) is used as a 100% natural binder. Guar gum or tapioca starch can improve adhesion further. No synthetic chemicals needed.

Fully food-safe binders
Step 03 — Product

Daily-use scrubber

One scrubber per day, 30 per month pack. Priced at ₹1 per scrubber — competitive with plastic alternatives, with zero microplastic shedding.

₹30/month · 30 scrubbers
End of Life

After 30 days —
zero burning, zero landfill

The month's 30 used scrubbers are collected together and decomposed through one of three methods. Select a method to see exactly how it works.

The simplest path:
home composting

Parali is pure organic cellulose. The binders (starch, guar gum) are food-safe and biodegradable. At month end, the user simply buries all 30 scrubbers in garden soil or drops them into a compost bin — no infrastructure needed.

1
Collect all 30 used scrubbers in the paper packaging bag over the month.
2
Bury the bag in garden soil (5–10 cm deep) or drop in a municipal compost bin.
3
Microbes break down cellulose + starch in 4–8 weeks. The silica residue improves soil drainage.
4
Nutrients return to soil. Zero carbon emitted. Zero landfill. Complete circle.

Community scale:
vermicomposting

Earthworms (Eisenia fetida) are highly efficient at breaking down plant cellulose. Community worm bins set up in apartment buildings, schools, or RWAs can process collected scrubbers — and produce premium vermicast fertiliser as a bonus output.

1
RWA or society sets up community worm bins (low-cost, locally maintained).
2
Residents drop their monthly bundle of 30 scrubbers at the collection point.
3
Worms break down cellulose in 3–5 weeks — faster than passive composting.
4
Vermicast output is premium organic fertiliser — can be sold or given to residents.

Urban scale:
biogas digester

Used scrubbers carry food residue from kitchen use — this is ideal feedstock for biogas digesters. Delhi NCR already has biogas infrastructure. Collected scrubbers are sent to digesters, and the methane produced powers local energy grids.

1
Monthly collection drive — take-back bags or neighbourhood drop points gather used scrubbers.
2
Transported to Delhi biogas facility. Food waste on scrubbers boosts methane yield.
3
Anaerobic digestion breaks down organic matter in 2–4 weeks, producing biogas (CH₄).
4
Biogas powers local energy grid. Digestate residue used as agricultural manure.

The 30-day model

How the cycle works

One month, 30 scrubbers, zero waste. Here's the full lifecycle from farm to compost.

1

Harvest

Parali collected from Punjab farms post-harvest instead of being burned in the field.

2

Process

Chopped, boiled, dried, mixed with natural starch binder and pressed into scrubber pads.

3

30 days use

One scrubber per day. Replaces plastic. No microplastics shed. Stored in paper packaging.

4

Compost

All 30 collected and composted together. Fully broken down in 4–8 weeks. Nutrients return to soil.
